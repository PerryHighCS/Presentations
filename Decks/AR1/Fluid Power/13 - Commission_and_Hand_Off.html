What Is Commissioning?

Before a factory accepts a new machine, engineers commission it: they test it against its requirements and fix what fails.

Today you commission your station, finish your user manual, and hand both to another team to test.

Part 1 is plan Day 14: commission against test criteria, revise documentation, peer operator test with the draft manual. Part 2 (from the Demonstration Day slide on) is plan Day 15.
Part 1  ·  Testing Day

Commissioning Checklist

Run your station through five cycles in a row. Check each item and record any that fail in your test log.

  • Fits on one base plate
  • Linkage moves without binding
  • No hissing leaks
  • The slowed stroke is clearly slower
  • Does its task every cycle
  • The printed part does its job
  • Pinch points are guarded
  • The shut-off valve is easy to reach
The checklist follows the Functional Prototype rubric rows. Timing both strokes is the clearest evidence of speed control.

Part 1  ·  Testing Day

Write the Depressurizing Steps

The operator must be able to make your station safe without help. Your steps must say:

1

Close the shut-off valve. It vents the air in the valve and cylinder.

2

Stop the program, and check that nothing moves when the trigger is pressed.

3

The tank is still charged. Write the steps your teacher uses to empty it.

4

Check the gauge reads 0 psi before anyone works on the station.

The brief requires explicit depressurizing instructions. Closing the VEX shut-off valve vents everything downstream of it, but the tank stays charged, so teams must describe your classroom method for emptying it. Step 4 assumes the gauge reads tank pressure.
Check

Write a Useful Troubleshooting Row

1. Which row would help an operator most in a troubleshooting table?

  1. AIf it breaks, fix it.
  2. BIf the pneumatics fail, check the pneumatics.
  3. CIf the arm does not move when you press the trigger, check that the shut-off valve is open and the gauge reads about 50 psi.
  4. DIf something seems wrong, ask an engineer.

2. Write one more troubleshooting row for your own station: “If ___ happens, check or do ___.”

A useful row names a symptom the operator can see and a check the operator can do. The other options are too vague to act on. Read a few strong Q2 answers aloud.
Part 1  ·  Testing Day

The Peer Operator Test

1

Swap stations with another team. Hand over your station and your draft manual.

2

The operator runs the station using only the manual, then depressurizes it.

3

Designers stay silent and note every place the operator hesitates or asks.

4

The operator tells you the clearest step, the most confusing step, and one fix.

Plan Day 14’s peer operator review. Glasses on and the usual safety rules apply: an operator who is unsure stops and closes the shut-off valve. Designers step in only for safety.

Part 2  ·  Demonstration Day

Your Demonstration

1

Say what the station does, then run three cycles.

2

Show the speed control: which stroke is slowed, and where the fitting is.

3

Show your printed part and explain how it connects to the VEX parts.

4

Trace the air on your schematic, then depressurize the station.

About five minutes per team. The steps cover the rubric: function, speed control, printed part interface, schematic and safety.
Part 2  ·  Demonstration Day

Everyone Explains

During your demonstration, your teacher asks each team member one question. Practice them with your team.

Trace the air for one stroke: which port fills, which empties, and where does the air leave?
Why is the flow fitting on that port?

The plan’s individual oral check, so each student can explain the pneumatic path and a failure mode. Ask a different question of each member.
