Layout

Draw Your Station on the Base Plate

Measure your base plate and draw its outline to scale from the top. Then add:

  • The cylinder, retracted and extended
  • Every pivot and the motion path
  • The product’s path through the station
  • The printed part, with its size
  • Tank, valves and tube routes
  • Pinch zones, shaded in red
Drawing both ends of the stroke catches the most common layout problem: a cylinder that hits the frame or the product before full stroke. VEX holes are on a 0.5 in grid, which makes a scale drawing easy.
Safety

Find and Guard the Pinch Points

A pinch point is anywhere two parts close together and a finger could be caught: a rod end meeting the frame, an arm swinging past its post, or two clamp jaws.

Design it out: keep gaps too small for a finger or too big to trap one, and slow the stroke with the flow fitting.
Guard it: add a shield or panel, and put the trigger and shut-off valve away from the moving parts.
ADE 7.4 and the rubric’s Safety and Operation row. A guard can be the printed part only if it also does work; usually guards are VEX plates or polycarbonate.
Check

Spot the Pinch Point

A station uses a cylinder to swing an arm up and down on a pivot. Which spot is a pinch point?

  1. AThe middle of a straight tube
  2. BThe regulator knob
  3. CThe air tank
  4. DThe gap between the arm and the frame as the arm swings down
The arm closing toward the frame (and the rod joint near the pivot) can trap a finger. Tubes, the regulator and the tank do not close on anything.
Schematic

Draw Your Circuit in ISO Symbols

Your schematic must show the tank, the solenoid valve, the cylinder and the flow control. Add the shut-off valve too: the operator uses it.

Receiver tankThe VEX air tank.
Shut-off valveThe operator’s air shut-off.
5/2 solenoid valveSends air to A or B.
Double-acting cylinderYour actuator.
One-way flow controlOn the port that empties.
The rubric’s Pneumatic Schematic row requires tank, solenoid, cylinder and flow control in ISO symbols. Symbols from Decks 6 and 8. A team with a regulator draws it after the shut-off valve, as in Deck 6.
Schematic

Check Your Schematic

  • Air flows from the tank, through the shut-off valve, to the solenoid valve’s P port (1).
  • The A and B ports (2 and 4) run to the correct ends of the cylinder, matching your real tubes.
  • The flow control sits on the port that empties during the stroke you want slow.
  • Both exhausts are drawn, and every symbol is labeled.
This checklist doubles as the technician’s view: the manual uses the schematic for troubleshooting, so it must match the real tubing.
Check

Where Does the Flow Control Go?

Your station should push out slowly and pull back quickly. Your VEX flow fitting slows air leaving the cylinder. Where does the flow control go in your schematic?

  1. AOn the cap-end port
  2. BBetween the tank and the shut-off valve
  3. COn the rod-end port
  4. DOn the solenoid valve exhaust
The rod end empties while the cylinder pushes out, so a meter-out fitting there slows extension only (Deck 8). VEX says not to put the fittings on the solenoid valve.
Design Review

Get Your Design Approved

Bring your layout, schematic and a sketch of your printed part with its dimensions. Your teacher checks:

  • It fits on one base plate
  • It has a linkage or movement system
  • Pinch points are guarded
  • The schematic is complete
  • The printed part does work
  • The part fits a 4 in cube
Approved? Your teacher initials your layout page. No air until you have it.
The plan’s design review and safety approval before pressurization. Record approval in the notebook so later build days can check it. Send teams that are not approved back with one specific fix.
CAD

Start Your Part in Onshape

1

Measure where your part meets VEX: hole spacing is 0.5 in, and VEX screws are #8-32.

2

Make screw holes a little larger than the screw, about 0.19 in, so a print still fits.

3

Keep walls at least 0.1 in thick, and check that the part fits inside a 4 in cube.

4

Name your Part Studio with your period and team. Prints take hours, so finish early.

The #8-32 screw is 0.164 in across; 0.19 in gives room for printer shrink and tolerance. Adjust step 2 for your printers if test prints show otherwise. The print queue opens on the first build day; teams export STL then.

Before You Go

  1. AExactly 0.164 in, so the screws fit tightly
  2. BA little larger, about 0.19 in
  3. CAbout 0.5 in, the same as the hole spacing
  4. DLeave them out and drill them after printing

2. What did the design review change in your plan, or what does your team still need to fix before it is approved?

Q1: prints come out slightly small and rough, so holes need clearance. 0.5 in is the spacing between holes, not their size. Q2 shows which teams still need approval before the first build day.
