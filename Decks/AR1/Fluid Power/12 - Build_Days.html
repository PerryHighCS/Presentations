Plan

Where You Should Be

End of build day 1

The structure is on the base plate, the cylinder is mounted and moves by hand, and your part is in the print queue.

End of build day 2

The circuit is plumbed, the flow fitting is set, the printed part is installed, and the station has run under air.

Ahead of schedule? Start your user manual: take the photos for its labeled diagram.

If printing needs more time, the plan allows an extra build day; the deck works for any number of them. Testing and the peer operator test come next, so teams should run their station under air by the end of the second build day.
Printing

The Print Queue

1

In Onshape, right-click your part and choose Export. Pick STL and set the units to inches.

2

Name the file with your period, team and part, then submit it the way your teacher shows you.

3

While it prints, build everything else. A print can take hours.

4

If a print fails or does not fit, photograph it and record the fix. That is iteration evidence.

Teacher print queue: decide in advance how teams submit (shared folder, form or USB). Check each STL against the 4 in cube before printing. The export also gives teams the STL file they turn in.
Safety

Before You Turn On the Air

  • Glasses on for everyone at the table
  • Your design approval is in your notebook
  • The mechanism moves by hand without forcing
  • Every tube is pushed in until it stops
  • The regulator is set to 50 psi
  • Hands are out of every pinch zone

Close the shut-off valve before you touch a tube, a fitting or the mechanism.

ADE 7.4, repeated every build day. The shut-off valve vents everything downstream of it, so closing it makes the station safe to adjust; the tank stays charged.
Notebook

Keep a Test Log

Every time you run the station, add a row. The rows become your notebook’s iteration evidence and your manual’s troubleshooting table.

TestWhat we changedWhat happenedNext step
1First run at 50 psiThe arm slammed up and threw the blockAdd a flow fitting on the rod end
2Fitting at 1 turnSmooth, but too slow to clear the next blockOpen it to 1.5 turns

Take a photo whenever something fails. Failures with fixes earn notebook points.

The example rows model the expected level of detail: one change per test, a specific observation, and a next step.

If It Does Not Work

If this happensCheck this first
Nothing movesIs the shut-off valve open, the tank charged and the solenoid plugged in?
It moves the wrong wayThe A and B tubes, or the solenoid’s plugs
It moves too fastIs the flow fitting on the port that empties?
It jams partwayDo both ends of the cylinder pivot? Does it move freely by hand?
You hear a hissPush the tube in fully; hand-tighten the fitting
