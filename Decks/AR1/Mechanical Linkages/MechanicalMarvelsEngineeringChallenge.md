# Mechanical Marvels: VEX Invention Challenge

## Your Mission

The world is full of machines that make our lives easier, from construction equipment and factory assembly lines to amusement park rides and everyday household appliances.

But the world needs something new. Something innovative. Something extraordinary.

**Your team has been challenged to invent the next mechanical marvel\!**

Using VEX components, you will design and build a machine that performs a specific task using at least **TWO different types of mechanical linkages**.

Your invention could solve a problem, make an everyday task easier, or simply do something entertaining and unusual. The possibilities are limited only by your imagination, your engineering skills, and the VEX parts available.

And there's another reason to make your invention amazing...

**Coming Soon: THE POUNCE\!**

Your invention will eventually face a panel of student investors at The Pounce. They'll be hunting for clever ideas, impressive engineering, and machines that actually work. Will your invention be the one they pounce on?

For now, your job is simple: **Build something worth investing in\!**

## The Engineering Challenge

Your machine must meet the following requirements:

1. **Include at least TWO different types of mechanical linkages** from the following categories:  
     
   - **Gears:** Transfer rotational motion, potentially changing speed, torque, or direction.  
   - **Belts:** Transfer rotational motion between shafts using a belt and pulleys.  
   - **Pulley Systems:** Use a rope or cable and one or more pulleys to lift or move objects.  
   - **Bar Linkages:** Use connected rigid bars and pivot points to transfer or transform motion.

   

2. **Perform a recognizable task.** Your machine must have a clear purpose beyond simply making parts move.  
     
3. **Make both linkages functional.** Each linkage must contribute to your machine's operation. Adding an unused mechanism just to satisfy the requirements doesn't count.  
     
4. **Use VEX components.** Your machine should be constructed primarily from the available VEX materials. Additional materials require teacher approval.  
     
5. **Be safe and stable.** The machine should operate without falling apart, creating unnecessary hazards, or requiring someone to hold the entire structure together.  
     
6. **Be demonstrable.** Your machine should be able to perform its intended task repeatedly so others can see how it works.

Your machine may be manually operated or motor powered.

## Phase 1: Brainstorm and Design

Before grabbing VEX parts, your team needs a plan.

Brainstorm several possible inventions. Think about tasks that involve moving, lifting, rotating, pushing, pulling, gripping, or transporting objects.

Consider:

- What will your invention do?  
- Why would someone want to use it?  
- What types of motion are needed to accomplish the task?  
- Which mechanical linkages would work best?  
- How will the two mechanisms work together?

**Design Deliverable: A labeled sketch of your proposed invention.**

Your sketch must show:

- The overall structure of your machine.  
- The two different mechanical linkages you plan to use.  
- Arrows indicating important movements.  
- Labels identifying major components.  
- A brief explanation of how the machine will operate.

You must have your design approved before beginning construction.

## Phase 2: Build Your Invention

Turn your design into a working VEX machine.

As you build, pay attention to the relationships between different components.

For example:

- How will motion transfer from one mechanism to another?  
- Are gears properly aligned and meshing?  
- Are belts or cables slipping?  
- Are shafts and moving components properly supported?  
- Can the bar linkages move freely without binding?  
- Are your connections secure enough for repeated operation?

Don't be surprised when something doesn't work the first time. Mechanical engineering involves experimentation, troubleshooting, and plenty of redesigning.

**You are expected to make changes as you discover what works and what doesn't.**

## Phase 3: Test, Troubleshoot, and Improve

Once your machine is assembled, put it to the test\!

Operate your invention multiple times and evaluate its performance.

Consider:

- Does the machine accomplish its intended task?  
- Do both linkage systems function as expected?  
- Is the movement smooth and reliable?  
- What parts of the machine are most likely to fail?  
- Could changing a gear ratio, pulley arrangement, or linkage geometry improve performance?  
- What could you change to make your machine more effective?

Make improvements based on your observations.

Remember, the difference between an idea and a successful invention is often the amount of testing and refinement that goes into it.

## Project Deliverables

By the end of the project, your team must submit or demonstrate:

**1\. Initial Design Sketch**

A labeled drawing illustrating your planned invention and its mechanical linkages.

**2\. Completed VEX Machine**

A working machine that combines at least two different types of mechanical linkages to perform a recognizable task.

**3\. Engineering Demonstration**

Show your teacher how your machine operates and identify both linkage systems.

Be prepared to explain how each mechanism contributes to the operation of your invention.

**4\. Engineering Reflection**

Briefly answer the following questions:

1. What is the purpose of your invention?  
2. Which two mechanical linkage systems did you use, and what does each one do?  
3. What was the most significant problem you encountered while building?  
4. What did you change or improve during construction?  
5. If you had more time, what would you improve next?
