The Rotating Field · Topic 01 · Three Phase Windings

Three Phases Take Turns, the Field Turns

Three winding groups sit 120° apart around the stator. Each carries one phase, so their currents peak one after another: A, then B, then C.

Phase currentswhite line = now

The combined field points toward the strongest phase. As the peak moves A → B → C, the field rotates at a steady strength, with no capacitor or switch needed.

The Rotating Field · Topic 01 · Why Slip Is Needed

The Rotor Chases the Field but Never Catches It

The field sweeps past the rotor bars and induces current in them (the amber glow). That current in the field makes a force, so the rotor turns the same way as the field.

Rotor speed
Field passes bars at100% of sync
Induced current, torqueStrong

At 100% the rotor turns with the field, so nothing sweeps past the bars: no induced current, no torque. A real rotor always runs a little slower. That difference is called slip.

Speed, Poles, and Slip · Topic 02

Synchronous Speed and Slip

Synchronous speed

How fast the stator's field rotates. It is set by the supply frequency and the motor's pole count.

Rotor speed

How fast the shaft actually turns. Under load it is always a little slower than synchronous speed.

Slip slip = sync RPM − rotor RPMsync RPM × 100%

A 4 pole motor: 1,800 RPM synchronous, 1,750 RPM on the shaft.

(1,800 − 1,750) / 1,800 × 100%

= 50 / 1,800 × 100% = 2.78% slip

More load means more slip: the rotor drops back a bit further so the field sweeps the bars faster, inducing the extra current it needs for more torque.
Speed, Poles, and Slip · Topic 02 · Calculating Speed

Frequency and Pole Count

Synchronous speed sync RPM = 120 × f / P
f = frequency in Hz  ·  P = number of poles

This is the generator formula from the first lesson, turned around. More poles means the field takes smaller steps each cycle, so it turns more slowly.

PolesAt 60 HzTypical nameplate
2120 × 60 / 2 = 3,600about 3,500 RPM
4120 × 60 / 4 = 1,800about 1,750 RPM

The nameplate speed is always a bit below synchronous speed because of slip.

The VFD · Topic 03

Running a Motor Slower

The plant receives 60 Hz power. A conveyor's 4 pole motor must run at about half speed today. How can we do that?

Change the gearing

A gearbox or pulleys work, but the speed is fixed until someone swaps parts.

Lower the voltage

The motor loses torque and slips more, drawing extra current and overheating.

Lower the frequency

Sync speed follows frequency: 30 Hz gives 900 RPM. This is what a VFD does.

A variable frequency drive (VFD) makes its own AC at whatever frequency the job needs. It can also ramp speed up and down gently and protect the motor.
Because a VFD builds its own output, it can even run a three phase motor from a single phase supply, as long as the drive is rated for single phase input.
The VFD · Topic 03 · Inside the Drive

AC to DC to Controlled AC

1

The rectifier turns incoming AC, single or three phase, into DC.

2

The DC link capacitor stores and smooths it.

3

The inverter switches chop the DC into fast pulses.

4

The pulses average out to AC at the new frequency.

The drive changes frequency to set speed, and changes voltage along with it so the motor's magnetic field stays healthy. It does not change the motor's pole count.

Starting and Stopping · Topic 04

Starting a Motor Without a VFD

Many motors run at one fixed speed, connected straight to the power lines through a motor starter. A motor can draw too much current for a pushbutton to switch directly, so a small control circuit switches a big one.

Contactor (M)

A heavy duty relay. When its coil is energized, its main contacts close and connect the motor.

Overload relay (OL)

Senses motor current. Too much for too long opens its NC contact and de-energizes the coil.

Start and Stop

Momentary pushbuttons: they only change state while you press them.

Why not a simple on/off switch?

If the power fails while the switch is left on, the motor restarts by itself when power returns, maybe while someone is clearing a jam.

The fix: a holding circuit

Start energizes the coil, and a holding contact keeps it on. Lose power and the hold is lost too, so the motor stays off.

Three Ways a Motor Stops

An overload relay trips on current that is too high for too long, which heats the windings. Fuses and breakers handle short circuits. An E-stop must be a safety rated circuit: a software stop or a VFD stop command is not an emergency stop by itself, and none of these replace lockout/tagout before maintenance.
