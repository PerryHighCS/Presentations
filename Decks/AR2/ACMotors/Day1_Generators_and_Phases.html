Hook · The Industrial Power Mystery

Home Power and Industrial Power

Home outlet

120 V on one hot wire. The neutral is the return path and the ground is for safety.

Industrial motor

480 V on three hot wires, L1, L2, and L3, plus a safety ground.

Both use alternating current. The difference is how many of the supply wires are hot. Think about three questions before we answer anything:

How a Generator Works · Topic 01

A Generator Converts Energy

A generator does not create energy. It turns mechanical energy into electrical energy by moving a magnetic field and a coil of wire relative to each other.

1 · Mechanical input

A turbine, engine, or your hand turns a shaft. Steam, water, and wind are common sources.

2 · Changing flux

As the magnet spins, the magnetic flux through the coil keeps changing in size and direction.

3 · Electrical output

A changing flux induces a voltage across the coil. This is Faraday's law.

The induced voltage is larger when the flux changes faster via faster rotation, a stronger magnet, or more turns of wire in the coil. Motors run on the same principle in reverse: current in a coil makes a force on a magnet.

When the N pole points straight at coil A, the flux through A is at its peak but is not changing, so the voltage is zero. The voltage peaks a quarter turn later, when the flux is changing fastest. Once the S pole comes around, the voltage reverses: that is alternating current.

How a Generator Works · Topic 01 · AC and DC

AC and DC Generators

Every spinning coil produces an alternating voltage inside the machine. What changes is how the coil connects to the outside.

Alternator: slip rings

Continuous slip rings keep each brush connected to the same end of the coil. The output reverses polarity every half turn: AC.

DC generator: commutator

A split ring commutator swaps the brush connections every half turn, so the output always has the same polarity: pulsing DC.

The commutator is a mechanical switch. This will come up again when we compare brushed DC motors with brushless ones.
Frequency and Phases · Topic 02

One Winding: Frequency and Period

Frequency is the number of complete cycles per second, measured in hertz (Hz). The period is the time for one cycle. In North America the grid runs at 60 Hz, so T = 1/60 s, about 16.7 ms.

Frequency of a generator f = RPM × P / 120
P = number of magnetic poles on the rotor

One shaft revolution is not always one electrical cycle. A 4 pole rotor passes two N poles by each coil per turn.

Frequency and Phases · Topic 02 · A Second Winding

Adding a Second Winding (B)

Predict: we add coil B one third of the way around the stator. Will its voltage have the same shape and frequency as coil A? When will it peak?

Same shape, same frequency, but B peaks later. The phase difference is the time shift between two repeating signals, measured in degrees of one electrical cycle (360° is a full cycle). Coil B sits 120° around the stator, so each pole reaches B a third of a turn after A, and B lags A by 120 electrical degrees.

Frequency and Phases · Topic 02 · Three Phases

Three Windings Make Three Phases

Coil C goes another 120° around. Now the peaks arrive in the order A, B, C, evenly spaced. That order is the phase sequence.

FrequencyCycles per second, in hertz
PeriodTime for one complete cycle
Phase angleWhere a waveform is within its cycle, compared with another
Single phaseOne alternating voltage
Three phaseThree alternating voltages, 120 electrical degrees apart
Three phase is not faster. All three waveforms have the same frequency. Three phase means three related waveforms that take turns peaking.

Why Three Phase · Topic 04

Why Industry Uses Three Phase

Single phase: total power

Power drops to zero twice every cycle.

Three phase: total power

The three phases add up to a constant total.

Steady power

A balanced load gets constant power, so big motors run smoothly.

Rotating field

Three windings make a field that rotates on its own. We pick that up next lesson.

Efficient delivery

For the same amount of copper, three wires deliver more power than a single phase pair.

Why Three Phase · Topic 04 · Your House

What Comes Into Your House?

North American homes get 120/240 V split phase service from one transformer winding with a center tap. L1 to neutral is 120 V, neutral to L2 is 120 V, and L1 to L2 is 240 V for the dryer and the oven.

The two halves are mirror images of one winding's voltage, so they swing in opposite directions. That is still single phase. It is not two phase, and it is not the same as three phase.

Factories, hospitals, and large buildings pay for a three phase service so they can run large motors.
Why Three Phase · Topic 04 · Real Generators

Real Generators Use Many Coils

Our lab used one coil per phase to keep things simple. Real generators spread each phase across many coils in slots around the stator. Coils with the same letter are wired in series, so their voltages add up into one strong, smooth phase.

Power plant turbine generator

Steam turbines spin fast: 2 poles at 3,600 RPM makes 60 Hz. Each phase is a long chain of coils in dozens of stator slots.

Hydroelectric generator

Water turbines turn slowly, so the rotor needs many poles: 40 poles at 180 RPM also makes 60 Hz.

Car alternator

A 12 pole rotor inside a three phase stator. Diodes convert its output to DC to charge the battery.
