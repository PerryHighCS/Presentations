Hook · AC Meets a One-Way Valve

What Happens to a Sine Wave?

In AC Circuits you saw that alternating current reverses direction 60 times a second. A diode only lets current through one way.

The 12 V in “12 VAC” is the RMS value, the DC voltage that would do the same work. The peak is 1.41 times larger: 12 V × 1.41 ≈ 17.0 V.

  • If this wave feeds a diode and a resistor in series, what shape appears across the resistor?
  • Most electronics, including PLCs and the LogiKIT, need steady DC. How could we get there from this wave?

Rectification · Half-Wave

The Half-Wave Rectifier

During the positive half, D1 is forward biased and current reaches the load. During the negative half, D1 is reverse biased, so the output is 0 V.

The 12 VAC (RMS) input peaks at 17.0 V, and the output peak is one diode drop lower: 17.0 V − 0.7 V = 16.3 V.

Rectification · Full-Wave

Why a Bridge Rectifier?

A half-wave rectifier throws away the whole negative half of every cycle. The load gets nothing half the time, so the average DC voltage is low and the output is lumpy.

What if we flipped the negative half instead of blocking it? Twice as many humps gives about twice the average DC, and the gaps a capacitor must fill get much shorter.

A bridge rectifier does this with four diodes, arranged so current always flows through the load in the same direction. In the Rectifier Lab you will find out which diodes do the work on each half.

Diodes as Protection · The Flyback Diode

Coils Fight a Sudden Stop

A relay coil stores energy in its magnetic field. When the switch opens, the field collapses and tries to keep the current flowing. A diode across the coil, pointed against the supply, gives that current a safe loop.

Troubleshooting · Diode Bench Diagnosis

Find the Hidden Fault

The circuit from Day 1 is supposed to be a 5 V supply, a 1 kΩ resistor, the diode, and an LED in one loop, and it has stopped working. Something is wrong, and you have 14 minutes of bench time to find out what.

1

Investigate. Each test costs time. Choose tests whose readings tell the causes apart.

2

Rule out. Strike out a cause on the Causes tab only when a reading rules it out.

3

Diagnose. Pick the most likely cause and the fix that actually cures it.

4

Defend. Tag the readings that prove it, then read your record.

Some choices are unsafe or bad practice. They cost time like any test, but they are never carried out, and they count against you.

EVIDENCE
Causes: diode reversed (key: in-circuit diode voltage, out-of-circuit diode test reads normal), diode open (out-of-circuit diode test reads OL both ways), LED reversed (in-circuit LED voltage of 5 V), and a 100 kΩ resistor (color bands or out-of-circuit resistance). The dead supply is a distractor that the supply reading rules out. The reversed and open diode look identical in the circuit; only the out-of-circuit diode test separates them. Unsafe choices: measuring resistance with the power on, and touching the diode to feel for heat.

Wrap Up

What We Know, and What Comes Next

Rectifiers

Diodes turn AC into pulsing DC. A bridge uses both halves, and a capacitor smooths the ripple.

Protection

A flyback diode gives a coil's current a safe loop, so switches are not destroyed by the spike.

Troubleshooting

Readings that differ between causes are the ones worth the time.
